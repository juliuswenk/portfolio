"_DOXA_ is an AI Agent based simulation that shows how statements change in a social network. It makes visible how original narratives drift over time.

To participate, visitors create an agent through a short BFI-10 personality questionnaire and intro-duce statements to the network. Its _Big Five_ scores assign the agent to one of four fixed, _Gerlach_-inspired profiles. They determine its initial placement and influence connections, forwarding likelihood and reframing. The profile stays fixed; the worldview changes through accumulated messages. Agents retell information in their own words and may send it to a connected agent. Each version, sender, recipient and timestamp is retained, allowing the path and current form to be displayed.

Strands Agents_ connects simulated agents to _Qwen3:8B_, run locally through _Ollama_. Prompts combine personality instructions, behavioural profile, current worldview, incoming statement and, where available, the immediate sender and previous retelling. A drift allowance limits change at each step. A _Python_ coordinator schedules agents and provides state through a _WebSocket_ server. Data is stored locally in _CSV, JSON_ and text files. The displays use _Electron, HTML, CSS_ and  _JavaScript; Three.js_ renders the network and information paths. One master computer runs the simulation, language model and shared data. Visitor interfaces and the network display connect through the local network. No cloud model is required.

The title refers an ancient Greek term commonly translated as opinion or belief. In Plato’s work, _doxa_ is distinct from _episteme_, or _knowledge_: opinion is uncertain and tied to the changing world of perception. Pierre Bourdieu later used _doxa_ for assumptions that appear natural within a social setting and are rarely questioned. When challenged, they become matters of agreement and dispute. _DOXA_ asks how a statement becomes credible when prior belief, social position and repetition act on it together. 

Special Thanks are extended to: Andreas Ingerl, Lennard Lev and Thomas Kemnitz whose guidance and support made _DOXA_ possible."
