# Improving Futurium

Edited portfolio copy. Original project descriptions remain alongside this file.

## At a glance

How can an exhibition make AI’s biases and framing tangible? Improving Futurium proposes a personalised character that accompanies visitors and later discusses the exhibition with them. I developed the character-generation and holographic-fan workflow within a team concept. In five days, we produced spatial visualisations and a working character demonstration for Futurium; the dialogue was prepared in advance, while the full exhibition remained a proposal.

## Context

Exhibition proposal and demonstration · Futurium brief · October 2025 · Five days

## Experience & intention

A visitor photograph becomes a 3D character that, in the proposed experience, accompanies them through information stations. On a holographic fan at the end, visitors would talk to it and adjust parameters such as temperature and intention.

Its likeness makes representation personal: resembling a visitor does not mean faithfully expressing their perspective. Changing its behaviour would reveal how the same information can inform, bias, or reframe an account.

## My role & collaborators

I developed the technological framework for character generation, animation, rendering, and fan playback, turning the team’s ideas into a demonstration.

We developed the spatial concept together. Conrad Lischewsky created the spatial visualisations. Andreas Ingerl led the educational project; Futurium provided the brief and received the final presentation.

## Outcome & learning

We presented an exhibition proposal with spatial visualisations, character creation, and fan playback. Dialogue was prepared in advance; accompaniment through the stations and an adjustable live conversation were proposed features. This was a concept and demonstration, rather than an installed museum experience.

I learned to turn a complex topic into a demonstrable concept within five days by finding a practical technological workflow.

## Key design decisions

A character based on the visitor’s photograph provides a personal reference for questioning whose perspective an AI response expresses. Adjustable behaviour would make framing visible. Connecting information stations with a final conversation gives visitors a way to revisit what they encountered.

## Implementation details

The character workflow begins with a visitor photograph. **Meshy** generates the character from the image, **Mixamo** supplies animation, and **Blender** renders the animated character. The holographic fan’s software prepares the rendered animation for display.

We demonstrated character creation and playback on the holographic fan. The dialogue interaction was prepared in advance because of the limited timeframe. Character accompaniment through the stations and an adjustable final conversation formed the proposed exhibition experience.
