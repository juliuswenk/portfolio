# Improving Futurium

## At a glance

What if an AI character looked like you but represented information according to someone else’s intentions? This exhibition proposal invites visitors to create a character from their photograph, accompany it through information stations, and adjust its responses in a final conversation on a holographic fan display. The aim is to question AI’s apparent objectivity. In five days, our team developed the spatial concept, and I built the character-generation, animation, and display workflow. Presented to Futurium in 2025, the result combined spatial visualisations with a working character display; the dialogue was prepared in advance.

## Short summary

*Improving Futurium* is an interactive exhibition concept in which visitors create an AI character from their own photograph, accompany it through information stations, and speak with it at the end. By manipulating how their character responds, visitors are invited to question AI’s apparent objectivity and explore how it can frame information according to different intentions.

## Context & timeframe

Developed over five days in October 2025 during *improvingFuturium*, a short-term educational project led by Andreas Ingerl.

Futurium provided the brief to develop a concept for an exhibition area. We presented our proposal to Futurium at the end of the week, supported by spatial visualisations and a character demonstration.

## Question & concept

How can an exhibition help visitors recognise AI as a tool that can carry biases and frame information, rather than an inherently objective source of reasoning?

The proposed experience begins with a photograph of the visitor, used to generate a personalised 3D character. This character accompanies them through different information stations before appearing on a holographic fan display at the end of the exhibition.

There, visitors can talk to their character about what it encountered and manipulate its responses through parameters such as temperature and intention. The conversation becomes a way to explore how the same information can be represented differently depending on how a system is configured.

The character’s likeness connects this question to the visitor personally: resembling someone does not mean faithfully representing their perspective. Changing its behaviour is intended to expose the gap between apparent representation and a system shaped by particular purposes. This makes AI’s dual-use potential tangible through its capacity to inform, bias, or reframe an account.

## My role & collaborators

I developed the technological framework that translated the team’s ideas into a demonstration. I used Meshy to generate characters from images, Mixamo to animate them, Blender to render the animations, and the holographic fan software to prepare and display the result.

We developed the spatial concept together. Conrad created the spatial visualisations. Andreas Ingerl led the project, and Futurium provided the brief and received the final presentation.

## Process & key decisions

**Making the question personal.**  
Creating a character from the visitor’s photograph connects the exhibition’s subject to their own representation. The character provides a personal point of reference for questioning whose perspective an AI response expresses.

**Making framing adjustable.**  
The concept gives visitors control over the character’s responses. Manipulating its behaviour is intended to reveal how an apparently reasoning-based account can reflect different intentions and biases.

**Connecting information with conversation.**  
The character accompanies visitors through the exhibition before becoming a conversational counterpart. The final exchange is intended to let visitors revisit the information encountered along the way and explore how the character represents it.

## Implementation

The character workflow begins with a visitor photograph. **Meshy** generates the character from the image, **Mixamo** supplies animation, and **Blender** renders the animated character. The holographic fan’s software prepares the rendered animation for display.

We demonstrated character creation and playback on the holographic fan. The dialogue interaction was prepared in advance because of the limited timeframe. Character accompaniment through the stations and an adjustable final conversation formed the proposed exhibition experience.

## Outcome & evidence

The project resulted in an exhibition proposal presented to Futurium, with spatial visualisations and a demonstration of character creation and fan display. The presentation communicated the intended experience through these elements and the prepared dialogue.

## Learning & reflection
