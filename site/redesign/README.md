# Merged portfolio prototype

Promoted to the main portfolio on 31 October 2026. Serve `site/` and open `/`. Public address: https://juliuswenk.github.io/portfolio/. `site/index.html` is the main document and uses `<base href="./redesign/">` to retain existing asset paths. `/redesign/` redirects to the main page while preserving query strings and project hashes. The original portfolio at `/legacy.html`, `/wireframe/`, and `Redesign/stem-test/` remain separate references. Old numbered project links open the corresponding new pages; omitted projects redirect to the previous site.

The five active projects are Carry the Computation, DOXA, Matrix Lamp, Visio Technologica and Veiled Panopticon. Their published copy and media come from the existing portfolio. The reading renderer and base layout are shared with the approved wireframe, including its section placeholders, CSS glass, uppercase section headings, name/JW transition and CV order.

Home loads the live collection once, with a **LOADING PROJECTS** screen and immediately available About & Contact. Direct links display project copy immediately; all five project pages also load the collection for their section models. A failed load or 15-second timeout leaves five clickable cubes. Late results are discarded. Each live object retains a real project link for Tab/Enter access; its focus outline follows the model. Mouse/touch dragging uses the physics prototype's six-pixel threshold, collisions and bounded release momentum. A click opens immediately without a hop or delay.

The physical world pauses while reading. Carry the Computation uses the original switch's eight meshes in six section groups, with each metal contact keeping its end cap: blue stem → overview, clear top → Experience & intention, spring → My role & collaborators, bottom housing → Outcome & learning, first contact → Key design decisions, second contact → Implementation details. These replace the six cubes in the existing scrolling layout. Each section shows the complete assembled object in the pose it had on the home page. Its assigned meshes use full-strength silver-blue X-ray; the remaining meshes use faint X-ray at their exact original relative positions and scale. Section models move into place over 850ms and return on JW/Escape. DOXA, VISIO TECHNOLOGICA and VEILED PANOPTICON use the same behavior with the groups below. Matrix Lamp highlights its complete LED in all seven placeholders, sharing the original geometry; only the original LED remains visible at home. Direct links load section assemblies without travel from an unseen home. Reduced motion displays and restores the models immediately; failed 3D keeps the cubes and readable content.


DOXA maps upper display → overview, lower display → Experience & intention, satellite displays with stands → My role & collaborators, main frame with center support → Outcome & learning, workstations with worktops → Key design decisions, and station structure → Implementation details. All nine exported nodes are used once, including their screen and acrylic submeshes.

VISIO TECHNOLOGICA maps outer shell with button → overview, lenses with optical mounts → Experience & intention, straps → My role & collaborators, face cushion → Outcome & learning, inner housing with controls → Key design decisions, and optical covers with accents → Implementation details. All fourteen exported primitives are used once. Group definitions live in `project-assets.mjs`; `project-parts.mjs` preserves the captured home-page orientation across every section and restores the original object on return.


VEILED PANOPTICON maps circuit board with indicator → overview, USB connector → Experience & intention, pin headers → My role & collaborators, power input with capacitors → Outcome & learning, microcontroller with socket → Key design decisions, and clock/reset/support electronics → Implementation details. `site/tools/split-arduino.py` regroups the existing disconnected components from `Redesign/stem-test/assets/veiled-panopticon.glb` into six named nodes. It retains every triangle, original vertex attributes, materials and embedded textures, and leaves the Blender source untouched. The generator validates the exact source revision before applying the component assignments. The input is the preserved four-node glitter export; review the assignments if that source changes.

Returning preserves collection positions and motion state. Resize reconstructs collision boundaries and hull scale from a snapshot, preserving rotations, normalized positions, velocities, sleep state and the settling timeline rather than replaying the drop. Reduced-motion visitors receive a settled collection. The original shaders, lighting, bloom, grain, FXAA and material settings are retained. GPU drawing stops once settled or hidden, including while reading without scrolling.

After a project transition settles, `project-stills.mjs` renders each complete section model into a small 2D canvas inside its own `.part-stage`. These canvases scroll natively with the text instead of tracking asynchronous browser scrolling through a fixed WebGL overlay. The shared WebGL canvas is hidden while reading, so scroll events and Safari address-bar height changes do not trigger full-screen 3D redraws or framebuffer resizes. Section size changes refresh the rendered canvases; returning removes them, releases their backing buffers and resumes live 3D for reassembly. Snapshots share the existing geometry and X-ray materials, use up to 2× pixel density for smooth edges, and restore the shared renderer's dimensions, pixel ratio and target after capture. Home rendering and live transitions retain their existing postprocessing; settled X-ray snapshots use the unlit shader directly without full-screen effects.

All five project reading views use the silver-blue X-ray material in `xray.mjs`, including every repeated Matrix Lamp LED. It computes the rim per fragment from the geometric normal and perspective view direction, with a faint interior fill. Front faces contribute additive light with depth testing enabled and depth writing disabled. The separation's eased progress crossfades cloned original surface shaders into the X-ray; settled parts share one unlit shader per project, without transmission, textures, extra geometry or new rendering passes. Returning restores the exact original material references and shadow flags. Direct links and reduced motion apply the X-ray immediately; interrupted transitions reuse the same state, and collection disposal releases the temporary materials.

The complete section assemblies are managed by `project-parts.mjs`. The selected original meshes and faint context copies share one centred section group, so their positions, rotation and scale remain exactly aligned. Each section contains every source mesh once; selected meshes have no duplicate faint copy. Unselected meshes share the existing geometry and one material per project at 4.5% of the highlighted X-ray's light intensity. Section models fit and centre the whole assembly, preserve the current home-page pose, and follow scrolling and resize. Faint context fades with the transition, hides on return, cannot intercept picking, and is removed on disposal. Context adds draws during transitions and snapshot capture, without continuous reading redraws.

On supported touch devices, the phone-and-shake icon at the bottom right requests sensor permission from a tap. It is dimmed when off and fully opaque when enabled. The first valid gravity-direction reading after enabling becomes the automatic zero for that user. There is no calibration button or required holding angle. During play, changes relative to that first orientation produce smooth, continuous sliding forces, with a small neutral dead zone. The relative frame supports upright and flat starts and follows screen rotation. It is preserved across play windows and reading; only disabling and enabling motion captures a new zero.

Deliberate acceleration adds bounded inertial impulses and starts or restarts the eight-second play window. Gentle tilt alone does not start or extend it. Expiry freezes the exact current positions and orientations. Reading, hiding the page, disabling motion or enabling reduced motion ends play; returning requires fresh movement. The force pressing objects toward the glass stays fixed, with no artificial lift. Dragging remains independent. Missing/denied sensors do not affect browsing. Tilt uses the gravity component of the existing motion readings, so it requires no additional permission prompt. Initial response strengths still need real-phone testing; sensor data is not transmitted or stored. Interaction reference: [Base Design — Gravity ball](https://test.basedesign.com/experiment/0g4f7z0ef5); this remains an independent Rapier implementation.

Asset credits and the existing Impressum are included at the end of About & Contact. Rapier 0.19.3 and its Apache 2.0 licence are copied from the test. Five self-contained GLBs total approximately 5.3 MB. Three.js remains pinned to the test's CDN version, 0.180.0.
