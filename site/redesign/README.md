# Merged portfolio prototype

Promoted to the main portfolio on 31 October 2026. Serve `site/` and open `/`. Public address: https://juliuswenk.github.io/portfolio/. `site/index.html` is the main document and uses `<base href="./redesign/">` to retain existing asset paths. `/redesign/` redirects to the main page while preserving query strings and project hashes. The original portfolio at `/legacy.html`, `/wireframe/`, and `Redesign/stem-test/` remain separate references. Old numbered project links open the corresponding new pages; omitted projects redirect to the previous site.

The five active projects are Carry the Computation, DOXA, Matrix Lamp, Visio Technologica and Veiled Panopticon. Their published copy and media come from the existing portfolio. The reading renderer and base layout are shared with the approved wireframe, including its section placeholders, CSS glass, uppercase section headings, name/JW transition and CV order.

Home loads the live collection once, with a **LOADING PROJECTS** screen and immediately available About & Contact. Direct links display project copy immediately; the four pages with real section models also load the collection for their parts. Veiled Panopticon defers 3D until home is visited. A failed load or 15-second timeout leaves five clickable cubes. Late results are discarded. Each live object retains a real project link for Tab/Enter access; its focus outline follows the model. Mouse/touch dragging uses the physics prototype's six-pixel threshold, collisions and bounded release momentum. A click opens immediately without a hop or delay.

The physical world pauses while reading. Carry the Computation uses the original switch's eight meshes in six section groups, with each metal contact keeping its end cap: blue stem → overview, clear top → Experience & intention, spring → My role & collaborators, bottom housing → Outcome & learning, first contact → Key design decisions, second contact → Implementation details. These replace the six cubes in the existing scrolling layout. The same meshes rotate, separate and enlarge into their sections over 850ms, and reassemble on JW/Escape return. DOXA and VISIO TECHNOLOGICA use the same transition with the groups below. Matrix Lamp repeats its complete LED model in all seven placeholders, sharing the original geometry and glitter materials; only the original LED remains visible at home. Veiled Panopticon keeps its section placeholders. Direct links load the parts without travel from an unseen home. Reduced motion displays the parts and reassembles immediately; failed 3D keeps the cubes and readable content.


DOXA maps upper display → overview, lower display → Experience & intention, satellite displays with stands → My role & collaborators, main frame with center support → Outcome & learning, workstations with worktops → Key design decisions, and station structure → Implementation details. All nine exported nodes are used once, including their screen and acrylic submeshes.

VISIO TECHNOLOGICA maps outer shell with button → overview, lenses with optical mounts → Experience & intention, straps → My role & collaborators, face cushion → Outcome & learning, inner housing with controls → Key design decisions, and optical covers with accents → Implementation details. All fourteen exported primitives are used once. Group definitions and viewing angles live in `project-assets.mjs`; `project-parts.mjs` applies the shared separation/reassembly behavior.

Returning preserves collection positions and motion state. Resize reconstructs collision boundaries and hull scale from a snapshot, preserving rotations, normalized positions, velocities, sleep state and the settling timeline rather than replaying the drop. Reduced-motion visitors receive a settled collection. The original shaders, lighting, bloom, grain, FXAA and material settings are retained. GPU drawing stops once settled or hidden, including while reading without scrolling.

On supported touch devices, the phone-and-shake icon at the bottom right requests sensor permission from a tap. It is dimmed when off and fully opaque when enabled. The first valid gravity-direction reading after enabling becomes the automatic zero for that user. There is no calibration button or required holding angle. During play, changes relative to that first orientation produce smooth, continuous sliding forces, with a small neutral dead zone. The relative frame supports upright and flat starts and follows screen rotation. It is preserved across play windows and reading; only disabling and enabling motion captures a new zero.

Deliberate acceleration adds bounded inertial impulses and starts or restarts the eight-second play window. Gentle tilt alone does not start or extend it. Expiry freezes the exact current positions and orientations. Reading, hiding the page, disabling motion or enabling reduced motion ends play; returning requires fresh movement. The force pressing objects toward the glass stays fixed, with no artificial lift. Dragging remains independent. Missing/denied sensors do not affect browsing. Tilt uses the gravity component of the existing motion readings, so it requires no additional permission prompt. Initial response strengths still need real-phone testing; sensor data is not transmitted or stored. Interaction reference: [Base Design — Gravity ball](https://test.basedesign.com/experiment/0g4f7z0ef5); this remains an independent Rapier implementation.

Asset credits and the existing Impressum are included at the end of About & Contact. Rapier 0.19.3 and its Apache 2.0 licence are copied from the test. Five self-contained GLBs total approximately 5.3 MB. Three.js remains pinned to the test's CDN version, 0.180.0.

Blue plastics now have fine silver-blue glitter embedded in their original pigments, matching the cartridge reference's flecked plastic. Shared 512px color, packed metallic/roughness and facet-normal maps export directly from Blender, with about 2.3% flake coverage. This updates the switch stem, headset accents, Arduino underside, LED resin and DOXA's frosted blue acrylic. Transmission maps preserve the LED's 36% and acrylic's 42% transmission between opaque reflective flakes; their clearcoat and emission settings are retained. Printed circuits and pale clear acrylic retain their finishes. The source is `Redesign/all-assets.blend`; its pre-glitter backup is `Redesign/all-assets-before-glitter-20261028.blend`. Local material scripts are `Redesign/tools/glitter-cobalt.py` and `Redesign/tools/glitter-translucent.py`; restore the backup before rerunning both in that order. Reexport with `Redesign/tools/export-web-assets.py`, then copy the five project GLBs into this page's `assets/` folder.
